Shopping List Organized by Carb Type Slow Carbs Definition: High-fiber, complex-starch, low-sugar carbs that digest gradually. They keep insulin steady and provide long-lasting energy. Best time: Morning + Midday. Foods: Steel-cut oats, lentils, chickpeas, black beans, berries, apples, pears ________________________________________ Moderate Carbs Definition: Medium-speed digesting carbs with moderate fiber and starch. They provide steady energy when insulin sensitivity is highest. Best time: Midday. Foods: Quinoa, brown rice, farro, barley, whole-grain toast, sweet potato (small portion), bananas, whole-grain pasta, corn/polenta, sourdough bread, whole-wheat tortillas, tortilla chips (midday only), low-sugar granola ________________________________________ Minimal Carbs Definition: Very small amounts of carbs, mostly from fiber or water. They barely affect blood sugar and help stabilize cortisol during the afternoon dip. Best time: Afternoon. Foods: Carrot sticks, cucumbers, bell peppers, celery, nuts, hummus ________________________________________ Low Carbs Definition: Low-starch, high-fiber vegetables and fermented foods. They prevent nighttime glucose spikes and support digestion and estrogen metabolism. Best time: Evening. Foods: Broccoli, cauliflower, Brussels sprouts, kale, zucchini, asparagus, sauerkraut ________________________________________ Protein Sources (full-fat dairy included) Greek yogurt (full-fat), cottage cheese (full-fat), kefir (full-fat), whole milk, cheddar, Swiss, gouda, mozzarella, feta, parmesan, ricotta, cream cheese, mascarpone, eggs, turkey, chicken, salmon, beef, tofu, tempeh, protein shakes ________________________________________ Other Coffee, aloe vera juice, apple cider vinegar, honey, alcohol (midday only; small amounts evening; avoid bedtime) ________________________________________ 3. Hormone Tutorial Insulin • Responds strongly to carbs • Controlled by slow carbs + protein + fat • Full-fat dairy stabilizes insulin when paired with carbs • Avoid fast carbs after 3 PM Cortisol • Peaks in the morning, declines at night • Stabilized by regular meals and avoiding late heavy eating • Spikes with alcohol, stress, fasting, late-night sugar • Full-fat dairy in the morning helps blunt cortisol swings Estrogen • Supported by cruciferous vegetables, fiber, fermented foods • Sauerkraut, kefir, and yogurt improve gut-based estrogen metabolism Testosterone • Supported by protein, omega-3 fats, zinc, magnesium • Full-fat dairy (Greek yogurt, cheese, whole milk) supports hormone production • Suppressed by excess alcohol and sugar • Optimized by morning or midday exercise Integrating Testosterone Support • Eat protein consistently throughout the day • Include healthy fats (salmon, walnuts, full-fat dairy) • Add zinc/magnesium foods (pumpkin seeds, almonds, spinach) • Limit alcohol and processed sugar • Exercise in the morning or midday for best hormonal synergy ________________________________________ Digestion & Glucose Absorption Timeline ⏱️ Timeline from Eating → Absorption in the Intestines 1. Stomach Processing: 2–5 Hours Food sits in the stomach while it’s churned into chyme. • Liquids + simple carbs exit fastest (1–2 hours) • High-fat meals can stay 4–6 hours 2. Arrival in the Small Intestine: Absorption Begins Immediately Once chyme enters the duodenum, nutrient absorption starts. 3. Glucose Absorption: 15–30 Minutes Simple sugars begin entering the bloodstream within 15–30 minutes of reaching the small intestine. 4. Full Nutrient Absorption Window: 3–5 Hours ~90% of nutrients are absorbed over 3–5 hours as chyme moves through the duodenum → jejunum → ileum. 5. Total Stomach + Small Intestine Time: ~6 Hours Food typically takes about six hours to move through the stomach and small intestine combined. ________________________________________ 🧠 Simplified Version • 0–2 hours: Food is in the stomach; minimal absorption • 2–3 hours: Food enters the small intestine → absorption begins • 2.25–3.5 hours: Glucose hits bloodstream within 15–30 minutes • 2–7 hours: Small intestine continues absorbing nutrients ________________________________________ 📌 Key Takeaway Blood sugar can begin rising ~2.5–3 hours after eating, depending on the meal, because that’s when glucose reaches the small intestine and begins rapid absorption.
